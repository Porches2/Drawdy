Highlighter Marker — Round 2

Focused on realism: multiply blend, high transparency, noise masks, dry-brush edges. Compare against V0 (flat).

V0 — Flat reference

No filter, no blend. Plain stroke for comparison.

A — Multiply blend mode KEY

mix-blend-mode: multiply on the SVG. This is how real highlighter ink interacts with paper — it darkens what's underneath instead of sitting on top. Very translucent.

B — Multiply + rough edges

Multiply blend + displacement for organic wobbly edges. Like drawing quickly with a chisel-tip highlighter.

C — Multiply + rough + streaky

Multiply blend + edge displacement + strong streaky alpha mask. Full marker simulation with paper interaction.

D — Multiply + dual layer glow + streaky core

Wide soft under-glow + textured core, all in multiply. Glow simulates ink bleed into paper fibers.

E — Chisel tip (flat edge) + multiply

Square linecap instead of round, simulating a chisel-tip Stabilo highlighter. Multiply blend. Wider, flatter stroke.

F — Dry highlighter (running out of ink)

Very high-contrast alpha mask with low minimum opacity. Creates visible gaps and dry patches like a nearly-empty marker.

G — Paper grain interaction

Fine-grain high-frequency noise simulates paper texture showing through the ink. Like highlighter on textured sketchbook paper.

H — Paper grain + directional streaks

Two noise layers multiplied: fine paper grain + directional fiber streaks. Most physically accurate simulation.

I — Saturated neon highlighter

Brighter/more saturated orange, higher opacity, triple-layer with strong streaks. Like a fresh Stabilo Boss.
